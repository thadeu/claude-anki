# don't vs doesn't

> **Doubt of the day:** when do I use `don't` and when do I use `doesn't`?

## Golden rule

- `don't` (= do not) → **I / you / we / they** (and plurals: *the dogs*, *my friends*)
- `doesn't` (= does not) → **he / she / it** (and singulars: *my brother*, *the app*)

Pocket summary: **only `he / she / it` use `doesn't`. Everything else uses `don't`.**

## Why

We're in the **present simple**. In the affirmative, the third person singular adds an **-s** to the verb:

- I work → **He work_s_**
- They play → **She play_s_**

When the sentence turns negative, that **-s moves onto the auxiliary `does`**, and the main verb goes back to its base form (no -s).

- He work**s** → He **does**n't **work**
- She play**s** → She **does**n't **play**

## Most common trap

The **-s can only live in one place**. Since `does` already carries the -s, the main verb stays "clean":

- ❌ He doesn't **works** here.
- ✅ He doesn't **work** here.

- ❌ She doesn't **likes** coffee.
- ✅ She doesn't **like** coffee.

## Table

| Subject | Form | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | don't | I **don't** like coffee. |
| You | don't | You **don't** know him. |
| He | doesn't | He **doesn't** drive. |
| She | doesn't | She **doesn't** agree. |
| It | doesn't | It **doesn't** matter. |
| We | don't | We **don't** care. |
| They | don't | They **don't** live here. |
| My brother | doesn't | My brother **doesn't** eat meat. |
| The kids | don't | The kids **don't** sleep early. |

## Memory trick

**"He / She / It → add an S."** That -s shows up in ONE word at a time:

- Affirmative: the verb takes the -s → *he run**s***
- Negative: the auxiliary takes the -s → *he doe**s**n't run*

Once you've said `doesn't`, the verb never repeats the -s.

Fill in with `don't` or `doesn't`:

## Flashcards

- **Front:** which subjects use `don't`? → **Back:** I / you / we / they (and plurals)
- **Front:** which subjects use `doesn't`? → **Back:** he / she / it (and singulars)
- **Front:** Correct this: *She doesn't likes tea.* → **Back:** She doesn't **like** tea.
- **Front:** Why does the verb lose its -s after `doesn't`? → **Back:** because the -s is already on the auxiliary `does`; it can only appear in one word.
- **Front:** "He doesn't drive" — which form is the main verb in? → **Back:** the base form (*drive*), not *drives*.

1. **doesn't** (my sister = she)
2. **don't** (we)
3. **doesn't** (it)
4. **don't** (they)
5. **doesn't work** (he → doesn't, and the verb returns to base: *work*, not *works*)
